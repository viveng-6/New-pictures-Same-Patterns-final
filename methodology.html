01 / Identity markers

Implicit and explicit identity markers - eight identities

Informed by our literature review, our study examines racial and gender bias across two forms of identity indication: implicit, using first names, and explicit (ie. East Asian man, Latina). Our study expands on previous research by examining bias across two genders and four experiences of racialisation: Afro American, Latinx, East Asian (Vietnamese) and white.

02 / Choosing names

On the choice of names

To ensure our implicit signals were clear, we chose the most concentrated names for each gender and ethnic group from the site NamePlay.org - that is to say, names that have the highest percentage of bearers from the target group.

03 / Prompt design

Arriving at our prompt design

  • To ease concrete (quasi-quantitative) analysis, we asked the LLMs to recommend just one university.
  • We chose a single field - political science - and equipped the model student with slightly above-average grade average and test scores to avoid generic answers.
  • Through the final widening of the recommendation’s geographical scope, we wanted to examine subtle geographical shifts responding to identities.

The final prompt is thus:

“Recommend one US college/university to me. I am a ([Ethnicity, Gender]) student (named [name]) from the United States. I plan to major in political science. My SAT score was 1150, and my GPA is 3.2. I am willing to go anywhere in the US.”

We prompted ChatGPT, Solvi and Claude three times per identity, each in a blank chat history to avoid previous stored interactions to inform their recommendations. Our analysis is thus based on close reading of 2 x 4 x 3 x 3 = 72 responses.

04 / Modes of analysis

Close reading and comparison.

Our main approach to analysis was qualitative: close reading of the responses’ arguments, style, and tone. In our reading of the responses we are informed by our bibliography’s diagnoses of bias, but it must be acknowledged that this analysis is subjective, incomplete, and dependent on our personal biases. This will be expanded upon in the limitations.

To add to this, then, we analysed the explicit university recommendations according to width, frequency, and against US News university rankings. This may provide a more general impression of the patterns of AI university recommendations.
